Neon Voyage: a free pixel-art rocket game

Fly a neon rocket through an endless asteroid field. Skim past rocks for points, grab shields and boosts, and set off a nova when it gets crowded. It runs in any modern browser, on phones, tablets and computers, with nothing to install and no account.

Play now

How to play

Computer
Arrow keys or WASD, or move the mouse over the game and the rocket follows it. P or Esc pauses, M turns the sound off and on.
Phone or tablet
Drag anywhere on the screen. The rocket moves as your finger moves, so your thumb never hides it. Add the page to your home screen to play full screen.
Gamepad
Left stick or d-pad to steer, A to start, Start to pause.

Scoring

  • Distance. Points every second, faster as the asteroid field speeds up.
  • Grazes. Fly close to a rock without touching it: points, and your multiplier rises by 0.1, up to ×5. Stop taking risks and it drifts back down.
  • Shards. Cyan crystals come in lines of five. Each is worth 25 points times your multiplier, and a whole line earns a bonus.
  • Smashing. Rocks broken by a shield, a boost or a nova score too. Every so often a meteor storm rolls in.

Made with Pixelbolt

Every sprite, effect and letter in Neon Voyage was made with Pixelbolt, an open-source pixel-art toolkit built for AI agents. Each piece of art is a small JSON recipe, and Pixelbolt turns it into exact pixels: no image model and no hand painting in an editor. An AI agent wrote these recipes, looked at what they drew and fixed them, the way Pixelbolt is meant to be used. The recipes are in the game's art folder, and art/build.mjs rebuilds every image from them.

The stars, nebulae and ringed planet use Pixelbolt's dithering and ramp shading, the score and messages are its built-in pixel font, and the image shown when you share a link is a Pixelbolt scene that places the same sprites. The game draws them all with Pixelbolt's atlas runtime. Try Pixelbolt in the browser playground or poke its live fluids.

Questions

Is Neon Voyage free?

Yes. There are no ads, no accounts and no tracking. The game is open source under the MIT licence.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes, in portrait or landscape, in any recent mobile browser. Drag to steer. On iPhone, Share then Add to Home Screen gives you a full-screen game.

Is my best score saved?

Your best score and sound settings stay in this browser on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Who made it?

Artem Skulimovskiy. The first version, drawn with plain canvas shapes, came out in 2024. This version was remade in November 2026 with Pixelbolt art, touch controls, working power-ups and sound.
